## Typography
- Use a condensed display family or similarly narrow editorial voice for headlines.
- Create hierarchy through scale, weight, case, line length, and deliberate line breaks.
- Keep supporting copy materially quieter than the headline.
- Use accent type selectively for urgency, numbers, categories, or one key phrase.
- Treat typography as a structural graphic element while protecting reading order and contrast.
- Allow typographic treatment to become more expressive in culture and anime directions, but retain a stable editorial core.

## Spatial system
- Use a disciplined underlying grid across formats, with visible margins and repeatable alignment logic.
- Build around one dominant reading axis per asset instead of giving every element equal priority.
- Reserve breathing room around the headline, logo, and interactive controls.
- Use modular blocks for labels, lists, buttons, and supporting facts.
- Let the image and type share the frame through intentional overlap or separation, not accidental crowding.
- Adapt the grid to the aspect ratio rather than scaling a square composition into every format.

## Image behavior
- Use photography, renders, illustrations, memes, and product imagery according to the editorial direction rather than forcing one image style.
- Treat the image either as the subject, as atmosphere, or as a proof point; make that role obvious.
- Dark editorial imagery can carry mood and urgency, while light product imagery can carry clarity and trust.
- Use image treatment, cropping, contrast, and overlays to unify disparate sources without making every image look identical.
- For culture-led work, expressive or humorous imagery can carry much of the hook.
- For product-led work, show the object clearly and avoid decorative effects that reduce recognition.

## Composition rhythm
- Alternate image-led, type-led, and hybrid compositions across a content series.
- Use asymmetry and cropped subjects for energy; use centered or spacious structures for premium clarity.
- Vary density according to information load: fewer elements for a single claim, more modular structure for lists and explainers.
- Use repeated markers such as numbering, labels, and compact metadata to create continuity.
- Build carousel rhythm through contrast between opening promise, supporting evidence, and closing utility.
- Let reel covers prioritize immediate recognition at small viewing size, while stories prioritize vertical reading and interaction.

## Accent system
- Maintain a restrained primary accent for brand recognition and high-priority emphasis.
- Allow each editorial direction to introduce a secondary accent family, but keep it subordinate to the brand accent and neutral structure.
- Use accents for signals—breaking status, numbers, keywords, controls, or focal details—not for filling every available surface.
- Pair vivid accents with black, white, grey, or deep atmospheric fields to preserve clarity.
- In culture and anime directions, richer fluorescent accents can expand the mood while retaining a controlled hierarchy.
- Avoid turning accent colour into a fixed content code unless it remains understandable across all formats.

## Brand invariants
- A recognisable condensed, high-impact editorial typographic behaviour.
- Clear contrast between dominant headline information and supporting detail.
- Consistent logo presence with disciplined, unobtrusive placement and sufficient clear space.
- A stable grid and spacing discipline adapted to each aspect ratio.
- One obvious primary reading order in every asset.
- Restrained accent use against a controlled neutral or atmospheric base.
- Editorial clarity: the topic, claim, and content type should be understandable quickly.
- A consistent sense of technology, culture, speed, and informed curation without relying on one visual genre.
- Accessible contrast and legibility across static, vertical, and carousel contexts.

## Non-invariants (vary with content)
- Image position, scale, crop, and degree of prominence.
- Text location, alignment, line count, and relationship to the image.
- The split between image, headline, supporting copy, and negative space.
- Background treatment, from dark cinematic fields to light product surfaces or expressive textured scenes.
- Accent intensity and secondary accent family.
- Information density and number of content blocks.
- Use of photography, 3D renders, anime artwork, memes, diagrams, or plain typography.
- Carousel slide structure and narrative order.
- Story interaction type and whether an interaction is used at all.
- Degree of asymmetry, overlap, texture, and motion implied by the composition.
- Editorial mood: urgent, humorous, futuristic, or premium.
- CTA presence, wording, prominence, and interaction format.

## Style directions on the board
- **Dark editorial technology** - Cinematic, high-contrast imagery; dark atmospheric fields; condensed white display type; a sharp warm accent for urgency and key facts; dramatic subject cropping; compact editorial metadata; strong image-to-type tension.
- **Internet culture** - Conversational hooks; meme or reaction imagery; hand-drawn or sticker-like marks; playful secondary accents; irregular annotations; approachable language hierarchy; faster, denser compositions with visible humour.
- **Neo-anime futurism** - Expressive illustrated or synthetic imagery; luminous night environments; energetic accent layering; cyberpunk or anime atmosphere; dramatic vertical depth; large declarative type balanced with immersive scenes.
- **Premium minimal technology** - Light or controlled neutral surfaces; clean product photography or renders; sparse composition; restrained accent interruption; generous negative space; concise utility-led copy; precise alignment and quieter hierarchy.

## Must not copy
- Any exact wording or copy appearing in the reference board, including its headlines, labels, captions, prompts, buttons, and supporting text.
- The exact imagery, illustrations, renders, meme frames, product photographs, textures, and artwork shown in the reference board.
- The exact compositions, slide order, crop decisions, content pairings, and format-specific arrangements shown in the reference board.
- The source board’s displayed logo or wordmark artwork if it is obsolete, incorrect, or not approved as the current NINJA identity.
- The board as a fixed template or a repeated visual sequence.

## Originality constraints
- Learn the mechanics—hierarchy, contrast, pacing, image roles, grid discipline, and accent restraint—not the source executions.
- Create new copy, imagery, artwork, crops, compositions, and narrative sequences for every publication.
- Do not reproduce any source asset, recognisable arrangement, or distinctive combination of elements.
- Validate the current approved NINJA logo separately; the reference-board mark is not automatically a brand asset.
- Treat every conclusion as a hypothesis from one reference board and test it against audience response, accessibility, and content performance.
